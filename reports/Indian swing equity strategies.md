# Testing Indian Swing Setups Despite Thin Evidence

Six long-only, daily-timeframe setups are worth paper-testing as Setups 15 to 20, but none arrives with trustworthy Indian proof. The best-quantified item is a **Supertrend-style ATR trend rule** (Nifty 500, 2012-2025, about 41% win rate, average win roughly 2.3x average loss at the 15-day setting). That study comes from a broker blog, ignores costs, and uses current index constituents. The strongest academic evidence is for **6-12 month relative-strength momentum**. It is a monthly-to-semi-annual effect, not a 2-20 day swing effect, and long-only versions have drawn down **66-75%** in practitioner backtests. For RSI(2) pullbacks, Bollinger squeezes, NR7, Donchian, VCP and gap-fill, the research found rules but **no Indian backtest numbers**. Costs set a high bar: **delivery round-trip statutory cost is about 0.22% plus a flat DP fee**, and with slippage (an unsourced assumption) all-in friction is plausibly **0.4-0.7%**. Cash equities cannot be held short overnight, so the book is long-only unless stock futures are added, and the repo has no futures security-id map. Architecturally, the swing book should be a separate engine modelled on `earnings_engine.py`, not new rows in the intraday option trader. I recommend shipping everything paper-only, off by default, behind a cost-inclusive, walk-forward replay that uses the production signal code.

## Quantified Indian evidence exists for only two families

The evidence splits into a few families of uneven quality. Trend-following is the only technical family with large-sample NSE numbers. A PhonePe Invest/Share.Market study tested Supertrend on the Nifty 500 over 2012-2025 across ATR periods 3-21 and multipliers 1-3, with about 65,000 trades at (3,1) and 15,000 at (3,3) ([PhonePe Invest](https://www.phonepeinvest.com/buzz/insights/i-backtested-200000-trades-using-the-supertrend-indicator-here-is-what-actually-works)). Win rate sat at **40.7-43.2%** whatever the settings. The multiplier drove payoff: (3,1) averaged +8.54% on wins and -3.64% on losses with a ~15-day hold, while (3,3) averaged +25.53% and -7.40% with a ~69-day hold. My arithmetic gives pre-cost expectancy of about **+1.3% per trade at (3,1)** and **+6.7% at (3,3)**. Only (3,1) fits a swing window. The source reports no drawdown, costs, taxes or out-of-sample test, and does not address survivorship. Treat it as a hypothesis generator.

The older stock-level evidence points the same way, with the same caveats. An XLRI working paper on 14 large-cap NSE stocks and the indices (1991-2005) found no simple moving average above a 50% success ratio, but a displaced 50X11 average reached about 60% success and beat buy-and-hold in 11 of 13 stocks; the authors concede ex-post rule selection ([XLRI](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/214/Profiting%20from%20Technical%20Analysis%20in%20Indian%20Equity%20Market%20-%20Using%20Moving%20Averages.pdf)). A Sensex study (2000-2018) found an RSI rule produced no positive return even before costs ([Colombo Business Journal](https://cbj.sljol.info/articles/56)). A 25-year broker study found long-only RSI rules worked and short or long/short versions lost money, but it used current constituents and no costs ([PhonePe Invest RSI](https://www.phonepeinvest.com/buzz/insights/the-rsi-myth-is-this-popular-indicator-worth-using-part-2-research-report)). The common thread is that long-only, trend-aligned rules look better than symmetric ones. The long-only constraint is therefore not a handicap for testing.

Momentum is the best-documented anomaly. Joshipura's NSE paper (1995-2008) found momentum profits at 6-month and 1-year horizons and contrarian profits at three years ([NSE paper](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/research/res_paperfinal223.pdf)). Sehgal and Jain find that CAPM and Fama-French do not explain 6-6 momentum on 493 BSE 500 firms ([IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eme/ijoemp/ijoem-04-2014-0046.html)). An Emerald study suggests a sector factor captures part of stock momentum ([Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/09727981111129327/full/html)). The practitioner numbers are sobering. Capitalmind's top-30, 252-day, monthly-rebalanced portfolio returned about 12.6% CAGR against 8.9% for the Nifty (2007-2019, pre-cost), but beat the index in only 6 of 13 years ([Capitalmind](https://www.capitalmind.in/insights/momentum-investing-basics-india)). Freefincal's 2006-2025 test showed Momentum 30 at 14.60% net CAGR with a **-70.61% drawdown**, and the high-turnover half returned only 8.51% with a -75.09% drawdown. The author reads the premium as an illiquidity effect ([Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/is-indian-momentum-investing-just-a-liquidity-illusion/)). Our universe is the liquid F&O list, so the premium may be weakest exactly where we trade. The honest reading is that momentum justifies a relative-strength **ranking filter** inside a swing setup, not a claim that swing breakouts earn the factor premium.

Event and flow signals are worse. The NSE bulk-deal study (2010-2019) shows cumulative returns of about 6.3% by the event day and 5.9% at day +7, so the gain accrues before disclosure and buying after the end-of-day print captures little ([IIM Calcutta WP 863](https://studentlive.iimcal.ac.in/sites/all/files/pdfs/wp_863.pdf)). A small PEAD study of 100 NSE firms (2014-2018) concluded the market is efficient to earnings surprises ([Trends Economics and Management](https://journals.lib.vutbr.cz/index.php/trends/article/view/541)). The research found no rigorous evidence for delivery percentage, F&O open-interest build-up, insider buying, stock-level FII flow, or any Nifty-200DMA, breadth or India VIX regime filter. Those are gaps, not negative findings. Two further caveats apply to the daily-reversal papers. An NSE 500 study (2014-2021) found its best daily-horizon portfolio was contrarian, but it chose the best of 1,177 portfolios and ignored costs ([arXiv 2302.13245](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2302.13245)). Reversals after large monthly moves appear to persist up to six months, though the sample period is unstated ([CEEOL](https://ceeol.com/search/article-detail?id=1054639)). That sits awkwardly beside 6-month momentum; my reading is that the two differ by conditioning on extreme moves, which the sources do not say.

## Statutory costs of about 0.22% per round trip raise the hurdle for short holds

Zerodha's published schedule is the reference. Delivery has zero brokerage for individuals, **0.1% STT on both legs**, an NSE transaction charge of 0.00307%, SEBI fee of Rs 10 per crore, stamp duty of 0.015% on the buy, GST on fees, and a DP charge of **Rs 15.34 per scrip per sell day** ([Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges)). My calculation from those rates gives about 0.2246% round trip plus the DP fee, roughly 0.24% on a Rs 1 lakh trade. The statutory parts are broker-independent, but Dhan's own brokerage and DP schedule was not retrieved and must be confirmed. Budget 2026 raised derivative STT from 1 April 2026 (futures sell side 0.02% to 0.05%, options premium 0.10% to 0.15%) and left delivery and intraday unchanged ([Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/budget-2026-stt-hike-fno-trades-explained)). A StockCalc article dated 17 October 2026 still lists the old rates, and I treat it as stale. The existing `charges_calculator.py` is option-only (flat Rs 20 per order, 0.15% sell-side STT on premium), so it cannot price these trades.

Slippage is the larger unknown. A figure of 0.05-0.15% per side for liquid names is my assumption, which puts friction at **0.4-0.7% round trip**. The consequence is concrete. The Supertrend (3,1) pre-cost expectancy of about 1.3% leaves perhaps 0.6-0.9% after friction, before the 20% short-term capital gains tax ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/long-term-capital-gains-ltcg-tax)). A 1-5 day RSI(2) trade that targets a 5-day moving average a percent or two away would pay a third to half of its gross edge in friction. That is why Setup 17 below carries a minimum-distance filter.

Structural rules shape the design as well. Delivery shorting is not permitted, so shorts need stock futures (lot sizes, monthly roll, margin) or are excluded, and T+1 settlement with BTST short-delivery risk argues against selling positions you do not hold. Stocks in GSM, ASM, ESM or T2T can be restricted to a 2% band, once-a-week trading, or a 100% additional deposit on the buyer, so stops may not execute ([Zerodha Market Intel](https://zerodha.com/marketintel/bulletin/454257/surveillance-measure-on-scrips-august-2026)). The F&O ban at 95% of the market-wide position limit blocks new derivative positions but not cash trading ([Tradejini](https://www.tradejini.com/blogs/what-causes-a-stock-to-enter-an-fo-ban)). For live use later, the SEBI retail algo framework (mandatory from 1 April 2026) requires a registered static IP, daily 2FA, Algo-ID tagging and market-price-protection conversion; a low-frequency daily system sits below the 10 orders-per-second line ([FYERS](https://fyers.in/notice-board/new-sebi-framework-for-retail-algo-trading-from-april-01-2026/)). Dhan lists data limits of 5 requests per second and 100,000 per day ([Dhan docs](https://dhanhq.co/docs/v2/)). Dhan's historical endpoint serves daily OHLCV from scrip inception ([Dhan historical data](https://dhanhq.co/docs/v2/historical-data/)). Whether those candles are split- and bonus-adjusted is undocumented, and survivorship bias is unavoidable with a current F&O list because the repo holds no point-in-time membership. The 2026 holiday list (secondary source, verify against the NSE circular) shows Oct 20, Nov 10, Nov 24 and Dec 25 remaining ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/nse-holidays-2026)).

## Six candidate setups, ranked by evidence quality and cost resilience

Ranking weighs three things: whether any Indian numbers exist, whether the expected gross move comfortably exceeds 0.4-0.7% friction, and how well the user's existing machinery transfers. Every parameter below is a **test value, not an optimised result**. Where a number comes from a blog or script it is marked; the rest are my starting points. All setups are long-only, enter at the next open after a signal on the daily close, and exclude names in GSM/ASM/ESM/T2T, names reporting earnings inside the holding window (the repo already syncs an NSE earnings calendar), and names under Rs 50 or below roughly 100,000 average volume (thresholds borrowed from the TradingView Indian VCP script).

| Setup | Idea | Entry rule to test | Exit and stop to test | Indian evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | ATR trend flip (Supertrend-style) | Supertrend flips up on the daily close; grid (3,1), (3,1.5), (10,3), (14,3); A/B with close above 200-day SMA and Nifty above its 200-day | Stop at the Supertrend line, trailing; exit on flip down; hard time stop at 20 trading days for the swing variant; gap-through fills at open | Best quantified (broker blog, no costs); XLRI MA paper |
| 16 | Relative-strength leader breakout | Top quintile by blended 126- and 252-day return (volatility-adjusted); close above prior 55-day high or within 5% of 52-week high; volume at least 1.5x 50-day average; above 50/150/200 MAs; Nifty above its 50-day | Initial stop 2x ATR(14) or below the base; trail 3x ATR or close under 10 EMA; max 20 days; sell nothing into a bulk-deal pop without a rule | Momentum academic (6-12 month); breakout rules are folklore |
| 17 | RSI(2) pullback in an uptrend | Close above 200-day SMA, RSI(2) below 10 (variant below 5); skip if distance from close to 5-day SMA is under 2x round-trip friction | Exit close above 5-day SMA; hard stop 3-5% or 1x ATR; 5-session time stop | Rules only (blog); mild support from daily-reversal paper |
| 18 | Daily Bollinger squeeze and middle-band trend | BandWidth at or below its own trailing 120-day 10th percentile, then close above upper band with volume expansion and rising 20 SMA; second variant buys a bullish close at a rising 20 SMA | Exit close below 20 SMA; stop at squeeze low; max 15 days | Rules only (Algotest); user's own history shows absolute BandWidth thresholds fail |
| 19 | Extreme-drop reversal | Bottom-decile 1-month return among liquid names, then a close above the prior day's high; Nifty not in a 10%+ drawdown | Exit at 10 trading days or close above 20 SMA; stop 2x ATR below the low | One abstract (reversal after large monthly moves) |
| 20 | Sector-strength overlay | Not an entry: tag each signal with its sector's relative-strength rank and test whether 15-19 perform better in the top third | Shadow tag only | Sector momentum paper (partial) |

Setup 15 ranks first because it is the only family with swing-horizon NSE numbers, a clean payoff structure (a low hit rate with larger winners), and entry and exit logic that reduces to one deterministic indicator. Its risk is that the 15-day (3,1) result may be a product of Nifty 500 survivorship and no costs. The grid should include the longer-hold settings only if a time stop preserves the 2-20 day identity. Setup 16 follows because momentum is the most replicated Indian anomaly, and the VCP and 52-week-high pieces add only a timing trigger. The only Indian 52-week-high paper covers option skew and reports no stock returns ([Saurav](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/686812/3b-Sumit-Saurav.pdf)), so the breakout trigger itself is untested. Setup 17 is cheap to test and fits the repo's RSI and ADX code, but it has the thinnest payoff per trade, so the cost filter is not optional. Setup 18 reuses the user's Bollinger knowledge but inherits a warning from the repo: Setup 1's absolute 5% BandWidth squeeze was always true on 5-minute bars, so only a relative percentile calibrated on real daily data is acceptable. Setup 19 conflicts with the momentum thesis and rests on one abstract, so it belongs in a shadow slot. Setup 20 is a feature, not a trade.

Several popular ideas should not be built as standalone setups. Buying after a bulk-deal disclosure, PEAD or earnings-gap continuation, delivery-percentage or open-interest build-up signals, insider-buy signals, gap-fill, and NR7 or inside-day patterns all lack Indian support. Delivery percentage is worth logging as a tagged, one-day-lagged feature (it is published after close), and the RSI-divergence idea should stay out because the user's own replay found no edge and Setup 3's divergence filter was shown to be "a filter in name."

## A separate swing engine reuses about half the existing plumbing

The repo review concludes that indicators (Bollinger, RSI, EMA, ADX), the roughly 213-name F&O universe with security ids and sectors, the Dhan client, kill switch, runtime settings, telemetry, notifier and market-hours logic are reusable. The intraday trader is not: `PaperPosition` is option-shaped (CE/PE, lots, delta-scaled option stops), and `paper_guards.flag_stale_carryover()` and `square_off_if_due()` deliberately close anything surviving past 15:20, safeguards added after the NESTLEIND frozen-quote incident. Forcing swing positions through that path would fight those guards. The better template is `earnings_engine.py`, which already runs a multi-day book with its own tables, scheduled exits, per-sector limits and an injected kill-switch callable. A `swing_engine` with a `swing_positions` table and a `book` label of "swing" in `strategy_decisions` keeps telemetry and the performance report usable without touching Setup 1 or 5 logic.

The genuinely new work is modest. The Dhan client has only `fetch_intraday_candles`, so a daily fetch (the SDK documents `historical_daily_data`) and a daily-bar store are needed; the recorder's `market_bars.db` could host a `1d` timeframe, but its 180-day retention is too short for indicator warm-up plus a backtest, and it is not in the nightly backup. Also new are an after-close scan, a next-open fill simulator, an equity-delivery cost function alongside the option one, a "Swing" settings group (disabled by default, with `swing_max_positions`, risk per trade, slippage in basis points, and earnings-exclusion days), and a UI panel mirroring Earnings. Three guards matter. Daily indicators need a variant that skips session VWAP and opening range. The holiday list covers 2026 only and fails closed, so 2027 dates must be added before 1 January or a trading-day count will stall. And the deploy gate checks `positions` and `live_positions` but not a new table, so a mid-hold restart is a new failure mode unless the gate learns about swing positions. Shorts would additionally need a futures security-id map, which `fno_universe.json` lacks, so they stay out of scope. Not verified in the repo: whether `indicators.py` exports ATR and whether the Settings panel renders new groups automatically.

## Validation must be stricter than the evidence it replaces

The user's established method (spot-based replay, pessimistic exits, then costs) carries over, with a daily-bar harness in `backend/scripts/swing_backtest.py`. Signals are computed from data up to day t only, with a test that truncates the frame and re-evaluates to catch lookahead. Orders fill at the t+1 open with slippage; a stop that gaps fills at the open, not the stop; when stop and target sit inside one bar, the stop is assumed first. The preferred design runs the real `swing_engine` against the replay clock with a fake candle provider, mirroring `tests/test_p3_replay.py`, so the backtest exercises production gates rather than a re-implementation. Reports should give Wilson intervals on win rate, bootstrap intervals on expectancy in R, profit factor, drawdown, exposure and turnover, per-year splits, a buy-and-hold Nifty benchmark, a random-entry control with the same exit, and sensitivity to doubled slippage and a one-day delay.

A suggested shipping gate is positive cost-inclusive expectancy in both halves of a walk-forward split, at least 100 trades (flagged by the harness), survival at doubled slippage, and a written survivorship note. Before any of that, one real Dhan daily call should be compared with known split and bonus dates to learn whether candles are adjusted; if not, adjusted prices must be built from NSE bhavcopy plus corporate-action records, while tolerating NSE's bhavcopy format changes (legacy broadcasts end 16 November 2026 per a secondary summary, [TeamLease RegTech](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/59714/nse-notified-regarding-the-streamlining-of-eod-information-disseminati)). Regime filters (Nifty vs 200-day, breadth, India VIX) should enter only as A/B toggles because the research found no Indian evidence on them either way. Past measurement lessons apply: `avg_r` was inflated by stops moving to entry (fixed by `initial_option_sl`), and one-day, 33-trade samples were treated as hypotheses, so the minimum-sample flag should be built in.

## Conclusion

The practical finding is that the Indian literature supplies **ideas, not edges**. Trend-following with an ATR stop has the best-quantified payoff shape, momentum has the best-replicated academic backing, and the popular mean-reversion and squeeze patterns have none, so the user's own validation will be the primary evidence. That reverses the usual order of work: the daily-data pipeline, equity cost model and survivorship handling are worth more than any single entry rule, because without them every positive backtest would be suspect. The cost arithmetic also favours the longer-hold setups (15 and 16) over the 1-5 day RSI(2) trade, which most needs the gross edge to survive 0.4-0.7% of friction.

Three open questions can be resolved cheaply before building much: whether Dhan daily candles are adjusted, what Dhan's actual delivery brokerage and DP fees are, and whether the Fyers CSVs on the user's Mac, resampled to daily, can cross-check the Dhan history. If Setup 15's expectancy vanishes after realistic friction and a delay test, the evidence suggests the swing book should pivot toward Setup 16's rank-and-hold structure instead of adding more indicator variants.
